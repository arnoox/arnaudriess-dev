The problem

Generated bindings compile, and can still be wrong.

bindgen and cbindgen do the mechanical part well. The bugs hide where Rust and C make different promises, and no compiler checks that.

Binding gate

The checks run in your CI. You call me only when needed.

Every audit or build comes with a binding gate for your pipeline. Your team regenerates the binding, and the gate tells you if it is still fine.

  • Pinned bindgen or cbindgen version and configuration
  • Layout and ABI checks against your real C build
  • Rules that flag changes to callbacks, ownership and unsafe
  • Half-day handover so your team knows why each check exists
GreenAll checks pass. Merge. No call needed.
YellowA risky area changed. Follow the rules, or send me the diff for review.
RedThe scope changed: new compiler, new target. Time to requalify.

FAQ

Questions.

What is a surface point?

Everything that crosses the boundary between Rust and C: functions, structs, enums, unions, callbacks, global state. Things where Rust and C can disagree count more: callbacks, ownership that moves across, threads.

How do I know my size before I pay?

You don't need to. The assessment counts the points and gives you the size and the fixed price.

What if my binding grows during the audit?

The price is fixed for the scope counted in the assessment. If new code comes in, you get a fixed price for the addition first.

Do you need our whole source code?

No. For the assessment, the headers or the crate are enough.

What if my team fixes the findings?

The report is yours, and your team can fix the findings itself. With the subscription, I re-check the fixes, up to two rounds included.
